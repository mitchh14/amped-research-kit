# Usability Test Plan Builder

This skill helps you build a usability test plan by asking a few focused questions and then producing a structured plan document.

Long session notes and screener templates live in `references/`. The session plan template used to generate output is at `references/session-plan-template.md`.

## What You Will Get

A complete usability test plan with:
- Study overview and goals
- Participant criteria
- Session format and logistics
- Task list with prompts
- Success metrics

## How It Works

Work through the questions below with the researcher. Ask them one section at a time. Do not ask all questions at once. If the researcher has already provided some of this information, skip those questions and confirm what you heard.

When you have all the information you need, fill in the template at `references/session-plan-template.md` and output the completed plan.

---

## Step 1: Research Goals

Ask the researcher:

- What are you trying to learn from this study?
- Are there specific questions you need answered, or problems you are investigating?
- Is there a decision this research needs to inform?

Prompt for at least one clear research question. If they give a vague answer like "we want to see how people use it", help them sharpen it. For example: "What specifically about how people use it do you want to understand?"

---

## Step 2: What Is Being Tested

Ask:

- What are participants going to interact with? (prototype, live product, concept, wireframes, something else)
- What stage is it at? (early concept, mid-fidelity prototype, near-final, live)
- Are there any known issues or areas the team is already worried about?

---

## Step 3: Participants

Ask:

- Who is your target participant? Describe them in plain terms.
- Are there any must-have criteria someone has to meet to qualify?
- Are there any people you want to exclude?
- How many participants are you planning to recruit?

If they are unsure on number, suggest 5 to 8 for moderated studies as a reasonable starting point for finding patterns without over-recruiting.

---

## Step 4: Session Format

Ask:

- Will sessions be moderated or unmoderated?
- Remote or in-person?
- How long do you expect each session to be?
- Will sessions be recorded?

If they are unsure on length, suggest 45 to 60 minutes for moderated sessions with 3 to 5 tasks.

---

## Step 5: Tasks

Ask:

- What do you want participants to do during the session?
- Walk me through the flow you expect them to follow.

Help them write tasks as scenario-based prompts, not instructions. For example:

- Instead of: "Click on the account settings page"
- Use: "Imagine you need to update your billing address. Show me how you would do that."

Aim for 3 to 6 tasks per session. Fewer is usually better for a 60-minute session.

---

## Step 6: Success Metrics

Ask:

- How will you know if a participant succeeded at a task?
- Are there behaviors you are hoping to see, or hoping not to see?
- Are there any quantitative measures you need, like task completion rate or time on task?

If they are unsure, suggest starting with: task completion (did they finish?), path taken (did they take the expected route?), and any moments of confusion or frustration.

---

## Generating the Plan

Once you have answers to all six sections, fill in `references/session-plan-template.md` with the researcher's information and output the completed document.

Use plain language throughout. Write tasks and criteria in a way that a recruiter or a note-taker who was not in the planning conversation could understand and use directly.

If anything is missing or unclear, flag it at the end of the plan with a note like: "Still needed: [item]."
